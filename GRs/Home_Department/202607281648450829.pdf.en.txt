Part 1 - Guidelines for Investigations into Death in Police Custody
1. Definition of "death in police custody": -
National Human Rights Commission Circular No. F.No.4-0/2016/DIG/NHRC
Dated 21.11.2016, the definition of death in police custody and death in judicial custody is as follows. : -
"Death in police custody" means death of a person when he / she is 1s in the 'formal custody' of the police or under 'constructive custody' of the police (means in physical custody of the police though the accused may not be formally arrested, such as as a person detained for interrogation without being arrested) then death of such person will amount to "" death in police custody. "" "
Thus, Cases where a person died while in formal custody or in constructive custody of police, then such cases will fall under the category of "death in police custody."
"Judicial Custody" represents a case wherein the custody of a person has_ been remanded to Prison Authority by a Judicial Magistrate. During his detention in prison (..e.
judicial custody), if a person dies because of any reason whatsoever, then it will amount to Death in Judicial Custody. It also includes cases where the inmate is medically ill or has died during treatment in hospital. In case where armed police escort from local police or district police is provided to jail officials to take the inmate to hospital, still the inmate is deemed to be in Judicial Custody and for death of such inmate the responsibility of intimating the NHRC will fall on the "Jail Authorities" and not on the local police. The police guard or the hospital authorities in such cases will have responsibility for safety, security and well being of the prisoner but the prisoner will continue to remain in the Judicial Custody during that period. Part 2: Precautions to be Taken to Prevent Death in Police Custody
1.Special vigilance before arrest and against accused handed over by citizens.
(As per BNSS 2023)
Mandatory Medical Examination-Sec 53, BNSS 2023: As per Section 53 of BNSS 2023, it is legally mandatory for any person arrested to have an immediate medical examination by a medical officer. If the suspected accused has any injuries or is unwell before he is arrested, he should be taken to a government hospital for a detailed medical examination before being officially arrested. The nature of those injuries and whether they are old or new, should be clearly mentioned in the medical certificate, so that there is no false sense of assault on the police later.
1.2 Civilian Assault Accused: - Often in theft, pickpocketing or other crimes, the accused have suffered serious internal injury or internal bleeding due to mob lynching or public assault in public places. Such accused should be immediately admitted to the hospital for medical examination and urgent treatment without being directly in the custody of the police station. Detailed records of external injuries on their body should be made clearly in the arrest panchnama and the Thane daily along with the names of the persons who handed over the accused to the police.
