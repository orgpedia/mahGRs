The Proposal: -
A number of development works are undertaken with a view to creating infrastructure in the state. The people's representatives are constantly insisting on taking up irrigation projects considering the importance of irrigation in the state. The government has undertaken a number of irrigation projects with a view to eradicating the backlog, utilising the state share of water as per arbitration, and providing irrigation facilities to the catchment areas. As a result, there are a large number of under-construction irrigation projects in the state. There are currently 234 under-construction projects underway in the state. About ₹17,000 to ₹19,000 crore is made available to the Water Resources Department annually by the state government to complete the project. Irrigation projects typically provide ₹16,000 crore for works such as Wd, hydroelectric projects, flood control, common plans (hydrological projects, quality control organizations, etc.), as well as provisions required for land acquisition, rehabilitation, Lok Adalat, Section 18-28 of the Land Acquisition Act.
The project cannot be completed within the stipulated time frame as this financial provision is not sufficient to meet the requirement of funds required for the project works, given the price increase in the project cost, as well as the huge increase in the amount of land acquisition. This has necessitated the creation of an additional source of funding for completion of under-construction projects.
The Cabinet in its meeting held on 07.08.2019 had decided to provide long-term loan of ₹15,000 crore from financial institutions for completion of 52 irrigation projects in the state which are under construction and are in advanced stage and can be irrigated in the next three years.

After that, the loan availability hat could not be completed due to various reasons.
In the intervening period, some of the projects in the above government decision were completed or included in the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana. Also, the remaining cost of these projects, irrigation potential and water storage generation have changed. The above Government decision was overruled by the completion of 75 incomplete irrigation projects under the Department of Water Resources and Rs.15,000 crores for improving the distribution system of 155 completed projects with a long-term loan of Rs.7,500 crores from NABARD in Phase-I. Approval has been granted by Government Decision dated 14/03/2024.
The above ₹7,500 crore was proposed to be used for completion of 36 irrigation projects of Phase-I out of 75 incomplete irrigation projects of ₹5,000 crore and 60 canals and distribution system improvements of Phase-I out of 155 irrigation projects of ₹2,500 crore, as per the government decision. Accordingly, NABARD has sanctioned a total loan assistance of ₹6479.76 crore for 36 incomplete irrigation projects amounting to ₹3982.76 crore and ₹2497.00 crore for improvement of Canal B distributions of 60 projects of Phase-I under Maharashtra Irrigation Reforms Programme and these works are in progress.
About 58 lakh hectares of irrigation potential has been created at the end of June 2025 on irrigation projects under the Water Resources Department in the state. This built-up irrigation potential has been utilized to the extent of 46 lakh hectares. The demand for water for non-irrigated purposes like irrigation as well as drinking water, industrial use is increasing day by day. Addressing the various demands of irrigation as well as non-irrigation purposes is a major challenge for the Water Resources Department while dealing with limited water resources. Apart from this, the department is facing challenges such as replenishing the untapped potential of the built-up irrigation potential, maintaining and repairing old projects with limited available funds. Various measures / programmes are included to address these challenges effectively. These include measures such as increasing water efficiency, bridging gaps in irrigation potential in built-up areas, supply through solidification, phase-wise improvement of old completed projects, etc.
The number of projects completed under the Water Resources Department is large. Very little funding is being made available to them for special repairs, extensions and improvements. Considering this, out of ₹7,500 crore in the first tranche, ₹6479.76 crore is required to be used for the Maharashtra Irrigation Reforms Programme.
Accordingly, the remaining ₹7,500 crore of long-term debt is also proposed to be taken from NABARD. In the interim, the remaining incomplete irrigation projects in this phase have been funded through government contribution. These funds have been spent on projects and some of these irrigation projects have been completed. Also, the balance loan assistance in the first phase as mentioned in the above paragraph is proposed to be used for the Maharashtra Irrigation Reforms Programme. For this reason, there is expected to be a change in the cost and number of projects as per the incomplete irrigation projects and the proposed planning under the Maharashtra Irrigation Reforms Programme.

Considering the RIT facts, without two tranches of the ₹15,000 crore loan, Reference No. The Government was considering a consolidated revised approval from NABARD for a total long-term loan of ₹15,000 crore for both completion of 57 incomplete irrigation projects (Form-I) and improvement of distribution system (Form-II) of 193 completed projects under the Water Resources Department by revising the list B cost of the projects in the above Government decision.
The 57 irrigation projects will have an irrigation potential of about 34.5 lakh hectares. Also, improvements in the distribution system of 193 completed projects are going to bridge the irrigation sector gap of about 4.51 lakh hectares.
A total of ₹15,000 crore of Digha Term Loan from NABARD has been sanctioned for completion of 57 incomplete irrigation projects under Water Resources Department and improvement of distribution system of 193 completed irrigation projects. dated February 10, 2026. A decision has been taken in the cabinet meetings and the government is issuing the following decisions accordingly.
The Government's Decisions:
In view of all the above, the Reference H.R. Baril Government Decision is being amended to allow NABARD to take a long-term loan of ₹15,000 crore for loan assistance from NABARD for the projects mentioned in the accompanying Form-1B2 subject to the following conditions.
1) Ensure that the interest rate on this loan assistance is lower than or equal to the prevailing interest rate (as compared to development loans raised by the state).
This interest should be charged only on the principal amount deposited as per the prevailing pattern of NABARD.
2) Out of the above ₹15,000 crore, ₹8982.76 crore should be used for completing 57 incomplete irrigation projects / project components and ₹6017.24 crore for improving the canal distribution system of 193 completed irrigation projects.
3) Under the Maharashtra Irrigation Reforms Programme (MIP), a proposal worth ₹5036 crore has been accorded consolidated administrative approval for "Improvement of Canal Distribution System" of 156 completed projects in the state, while under the Maharashtra Irrigation Reforms Programme (MIP), a proposal worth ₹37 crore for a total of newly included HATA projects is being accorded consolidated administrative approval and the project report should be approved at the Water Resources Department level within the aforesaid cost limit by preparing a Project-wise Detailed Project Report (DPR).
