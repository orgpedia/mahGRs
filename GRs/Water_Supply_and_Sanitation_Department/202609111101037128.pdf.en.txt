Government Decisions: - Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation, Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India. Under this programme, Water Quality Monitoring & Surveillance Programme is being implemented in the state to provide quality drinking water to the rural population.

Considering the fact that regular purification of water in rural areas of the State and control and monitoring of water quality is essential for prevention of epidemics, Government Decision Reference No. 3, D. Under the 20/07/2022, guidelines have been issued for effective implementation of quality control and survey programme under Jal Jeevan Mission. It directs that all drinking water sources in the village be chemically tested once in a year (at least two samples) with household tap water supply (FHTC) and biopsy twice a year in the laboratory. Accordingly, an estimated 7 lakh water samples (chemical and biological) are tested annually.
The guidelines of the Water Quality Survey Programme under Sections 4 and 5 of the Central Government have been received, as per which the water available in every village household, schools, anganwadis, public distribution tanks, water purification stations, water samples of the sources have been suggested for testing. Considering the number of household water, schools, anganwadis, public distribution tanks, water treatment plants, sources in the state, an estimated 3.5 million water samples are required to be tested annually for chemical testing and approximately 3 million for biological testing. In order to effectively implement the water quality programme as per the guidelines of the Central Government, it is necessary to consider options such as strengthening of the operational laboratory to meet the testing target, testing of various equipment and testing with the help of private laboratories, etc.
Under the Jal Jeevan Mission programme, the implementation agencies are Ground Water Monitoring and Development System, Zilla Parishad and Maharashtra Jeevan Authority to implement the Water Quality Monitoring and Surveillance Programme in the state. However, since all of these systems make separate purchases for different things, having a uniformity in them is likely to result in wastage of government funds. It is proposed to constitute a Procurement Committee under the Chairmanship of Member Secretary, Maharashtra Jeevan Authority to coordinate the procurement process for the implementation of Quality Control and Survey Programme through ARTA implementing agencies for quality procurement of various plants, equipments, chemicals, glassware, laboratory materials, Field Testing Kits (FTKs), etc., and essential services for water quality testing such as Co., manpower, hiring vehicles, water testing from external sources, etc.
