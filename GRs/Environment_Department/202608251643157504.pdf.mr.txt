Preamble :
Consequent upon the insertion of Section 21A in the Air Act, 1981 and Section 27A in the Water Act, 1974 by the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023, the Ministry of
Environment, Forest and Climate Change issued the Control of Air Pollution and Control of
Water Pollution (Grant, Refusal or Cancellation of Consent) Guidelines, 2025, which were
thereafter amended by the Control of Air Pollution (Grant, Refusal or Cancellation of Consent)
Amendment Guidelines, 2026 and Water Pollution (Grant, Refusal or Cancellation of Consent)
Amendment Guidelines, 2026 (in force from 27.01.2026). Every State Board is required to act
in accordance with the Guidelines as so amended.
The Air Pollution (Grant, Refusal or Cancellation of Consent) Amendment Guidelines,
2026 and Water Pollution (Grant, Refusal or Cancellation of Consent) Amendment Guidelines,
2026 effect the following material changes relevant to fee fixation : (a) the Consent to Operate,
once granted, remains valid until it is cancelled, and the earlier category-wise validity periods,
the provision for renewal, the 5% early-renewal rebate and the late-fee schedule have been
removed; (b) the State Government / Union Territory Administration may prescribe a one-time Consent to Operate fee for any period from 5 to 25 years as applied for by the project proponent,
# Page 2
and a fresh one-time fee is payable for any extension of the period after its expiry; (c) a uniform definition of “capital investment” 15 introduced in Schedule II; (त) a single-step Consolidated
Consent and Authorization is provided, covering the Air Act, the Water Act and authorizations
under the waste management rules under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; and (e) Micro
and Small enterprises located in duly notified industrial estates / areas are granted deemed
Consent to Establish upon submission of a self-certified Form-I.
The Guidelines continue to provide that the consent fee shall not exceed the upper limit specified in the Second Schedule; that the State may prescribe any lower fee with no mandated minimum; that the fee shall not be increased by more than ten per cent of the existing fee and not more than once in two years; and that the fee may be reduced any number of times.
The Guidelines, as amended in 2026, continue to provide that the fee specified in the
Second Schedule is the upper limit (ceiling); that the State Government may prescribe any lower amount of fee with no mandated minimum; and that the fee may be reduced any number of times.
The State is therefore entitled, as a matter of policy, to fix a fee equal to or lower than the Second
Schedule rate.
As per the proposal of Maharashtra Pollution Control Board the matter being under the consideration of Government, the following decision 1s taken.
GOVERNMENT RESOLUTION :
The rates of consent fee charged by the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board for grant
of Consolidated Consent and Authorization under the Air Act, the Water Act and the applicable
waste management rules are hereby revised as set out below, with effect from the date of issue
of this Resolution. A single combined fee 15 levied in lieu of separate fees under the Air Act and
the Water Act.
1. Definition of Capital Investment
For the purpose of computing the consent fee, “capital investment” means the total value of fixed assets, namely land, buildings (including staff quarters and guest houses), industrial
premises, plant and machinery, and pollution-control equipment, but excluding operation and
maintenance expenses and other revenue expenditure. In the case of leased premises, ten times the annual lease value or the guidance value of the property / land, whichever is higher, shall be
treated as the cost of land for fee computation. (As per Schedule I of the Guidelines, as amended in 2026.)

Slab mechanism: the maximum per-year fee of the preceding slab serves as the base for the next slab, and the Scale Factor of the next slab applies on the difference amount, i.e. CF, =
max. fee of preceding slab + {(difference of CI) x SF x PIF}.
(b) Category-wise rebate.
On the per-year combined fee so computed, a rebate 1s allowed of 50% for Green, 30% for Orange and 20% for Red category. The effective minimum per-year combined fee is Rs. 2,500 (Green), Rs. 5,250 (Orange) and Rs. 8,000 (Red). The slab-wise per-year combined fee is at Annexure-I .
(c) One-time Consent to Operate fee.
Consent to Operate is granted for a one-time fee for any period of 5 to 25 years as applied for by the project proponent, and once granted remains valid until cancelled. The one-time Consent to
Operate fee shall be —
One-time CTO fee = CF, (per-year combined fee, after rebate) x N
where N is the number of years (5 to 25) of the consent period applied for. A fresh one-time fee, computed on the fee prevailing at the time, shall be payable for any extension of the period after its expiry.
(d) Consent to Establish fee.
The Consent to Establish fee (one-time) shall be equal to one year’s combined fee CF, computed
as above (without category rebate for Building and Construction projects under Categories 10.0
and 10.1). For Micro and Small enterprises located in duly notified industrial estates / areas,
Consent to Establish shall be deemed to be granted upon submission of a self-certified Form-I, without fee.

5. Consolidated Consent, Validity and General Provisions
(a) A single-step Consolidated Consent and Authorization shall be granted under the Air Act,
the Water Act and the waste management rules under the Environment (Protection) Act,
1986 Gncluding the Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016, the Bio-Medical Waste
Management Rules, 2016, the Construction and Demolition Waste Management Rules,
2016 and other applicable rules), through the unified online portal once operational.
(b) Consent to Operate, once granted, shall remain valid until cancelled in accordance with
the Guidelines. A fresh one-time fee shall be payable only for extension of the period after
expiry of the period for which the one-time fee was paid.
(c) The fee prescribed herein shall not exceed the upper limit of the Second Schedule of the Guidelines. Any future increase shall not exceed ten per cent of the existing fee and shall not be made more than once in two years; the fee may be reduced any number of times.
# Page 6
(d) Five per cent of the consent fee shall be credited to the Central Pollution Control Board as service fee in accordance with Section 33 of the Water Act, 1974 / the said Guidelines.
(e) Verification and inspection may be carried out by officers of the Board or by a Registered Environment Auditor engaged by the project proponent, in accordance with the Guidelines.
(f) The circular dated 12.07.2021 regarding penal fees for occupiers violating the combined-
consent regime stands cancelled, as resolved by the Board.
(¢) The Member Secretary, Maharashtra Pollution Control Board, is authorized to issue
consequential circulars, configure the concerned online portal accordingly, and take all
steps necessary to give effect to this Resolution. The revised rates take effect from the date of issue of this Resolution.
